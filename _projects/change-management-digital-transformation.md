## Why this project matters to me

The strategy made sense on paper. The value was lost in the gap between the plan and daily operations. Since this project, I look at every transformation with one question: **does this change actually remove work, or does it just move it somewhere less visible?**

## The frameworks we used, and what each one showed

| Framework | What it asks | What it revealed here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lewin's change model | Has the organisation unfrozen old habits, changed, and refrozen the new way? | The change phase was running, but nothing was refreezing: people kept old manual steps "just in case" |
| McKinsey 7S | Do strategy, structure, systems, shared values, style, staff and skills fit together? | Systems moved ahead while structure, shared values and leadership style lagged behind |
| Kotter's 8 steps | Is there urgency, a guiding team, a vision, communication and quick wins? | No shared vision and one-way communication, so quick wins were not visible to staff |
| Schein's culture model | What unwritten assumptions shape behaviour? | A weak error culture: people hid problems instead of raising them early |

## How the interviews worked

Each interview lasted 60 to 75 minutes and followed a semi-structured guide, so we could compare answers while still following up on what people raised themselves. We coded every interview against the frameworks, then combined the codes into master themes across all interviews. The most important pattern only appeared in that comparison: **every group described the same gap between the new system and the way work was really done**, just from different sides.

## What we recommended

- **A clear transformation roadmap**, so employees understand the direction and the order of changes.
- **Cross-functional process ownership**, to break silos where one process touches several departments.
- **A communication loop**, replacing one-way intranet updates with regular two-way feedback.
- **Leadership and error culture:** learning reviews after milestones that ask what worked, what did not and what to adjust, without blaming individuals.
- **A realistic implementation timeline** that avoids change overload.
