**Part 12 of my series on why half of new Swiss companies close within five years.**

## The answer in one sentence

New Swiss companies mostly fail because of **how they start and which market they enter**, not because of where they are or how much money they raise.

## Recommendations

- **Founders:** do not start alone; start with a partner or a first hire. Choose a niche with a barrier (skills, licence, trust). Test demand before signing long leases or large contracts.
- **SME owners:** put AI on back-office tasks first, with data rules in place. Track cash runway and unit costs monthly.
- **Investors:** look past AI labels and ask what part of the value survives when AI tools get cheaper.
- **Cantons:** measure economic health by survival and jobs, not registrations, and support years two and three, not only the founding.

## An agile playbook for small firms

1. **Pre-mortem:** imagine the big decision failed a year from now and list why. Fix the top three reasons first.
2. **Smallest test first:** a pop-up instead of a lease, a pilot instead of a rollout.
3. **Short cycles:** review every two to four weeks and decide to continue, change or stop.
4. **Kill criteria in advance:** decide before starting what result means stop.

That is where my Scrum background meets consulting: **turn one big, irreversible bet into many small, reversible experiments.**
