**Part 8 of my series on why half of new Swiss companies close within five years.**

| Study | Who was asked | Success means | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Google Cloud | Firms already using AI | Any ROI | 74% |
| IBM | 2,000 CEOs | The ROI they expected | 25% |
| McKinsey | 1,993 participants | Any profit effect | 39% |
| S&P Global | Companies in 2025 | Projects kept | 42% dropped most |
| MIT NANDA | 300+ initiatives | Measurable P&L effect in six months | 5% |

## Five reasons they disagree

1. **Who is in the sample.** Asking only companies that deployed AI leaves out every project that died earlier.
2. **What counts as success.** "Any ROI" is far easier than "the ROI we expected".
3. **The time window.** A project can fail at six months and succeed at eighteen.
4. **Who answers.** Executives rate projects they sponsored themselves.
5. **Who publishes.** Some publishers sell AI products or AI transformation work.

## The hidden denominator

Inside McKinsey's own study: of every 100 organisations, **88 use AI, 39 see any effect on profit, and 6 see a large effect**. All three are true at once. Which one makes the headline depends on the writer.

## Why even a 25 to 30% failure rate matters

A large company can run 40 pilots and accept that a third fail. A small firm usually gets one bet. **A 70% success rate is fine for many small experiments and dangerous for one large, irreversible bet.**
