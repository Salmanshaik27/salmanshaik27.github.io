Data tells you what happened; strategy has to think about what could happen next. Scenario planning, made famous by Shell in the 1970s, does not try to predict a single future. It picks the two forces that matter most and are most uncertain, crosses them, and asks what each of the four resulting worlds would mean.

For new Swiss companies, the first force is **how fast AI spreads**. The share of SMEs consciously using AI rose from 22% to 34% in a single year.[^axa] The second is **how turbulent the outside world becomes**. US tariffs on Swiss goods went from 39% in August 2025 to a 15% ceiling, then a flat 10% from February 2026, with up to 15% on pharmaceuticals from April 2026.[^es] Energy prices and the strength of the franc add to the uncertainty.

{% include diagrams/scenario-matrix.html %}

My base case is the **AI shake-out**: AI adoption keeps accelerating while the economy grows moderately, as SECO's raised forecast of 1.7% growth for 2026 suggests.[^seco] In that world, cheap AI tools keep foundings high in consulting and marketing, many of these one-person firms close within two or three years, and IT service companies that sell hours are pushed to sell outcomes instead. The wild card is cost turbulence. If tariffs, energy prices or a stronger franc turn against Switzerland just as AI pressure grows, the **double shock** follows, and the most exposed sectors could fall below 50% survival.

> Scenarios are not forecasts. Their value lies in what they tell you to prepare for, and which signposts to watch.
{: .note}

## If this happens, then what

| Trigger | First effect | Who loses, who wins | Signpost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SNB rate rise of 0.25 points | Slightly dearer borrowing | Small effect overall | SNB decision[^kof] |
| Higher US pharma tariffs | Big pharma cuts costs | Basel suppliers and labs lose | US rulings, pharma results |
| A stronger franc | More shopping and holidays abroad | Hospitality, retail, exporters lose | EUR/CHF |
| AI agents mature quickly | Less paid office and basic IT work | AI-native firms win | BFS foundings by industry |
| AI valuations correct | Fewer AI rounds | Capital returns to non-AI startups | AI share of venture capital |
| Bankruptcy-law effect fades | Bankruptcy numbers fall | Headlines call it a recovery | Creditreform, D&B |

*Likelihoods and effects are my own judgement based on the study.*

The chains connect. A stronger franc, high energy prices and AI pressure can hit the same small firm in the same year, and that compounding is what turns a difficult year into a closure. The full scenario analysis is in [sections 14 and 15 of the study](/work/swiss-startup-survival/#s14).
