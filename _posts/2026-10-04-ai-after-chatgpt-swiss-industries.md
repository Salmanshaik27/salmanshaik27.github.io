**Part 7 of my series on why half of new Swiss companies close within five years.**

A note first: the direct effect of AI on five-year survival **cannot be proven yet**. The data that would show it, the 2023 founding year at year five, arrives around 2030. What follows is strong circumstantial evidence.

## Where AI is boosting

- AI startups went from **5% to 32% of Swiss venture capital** in two years (EY Startup Barometer 2026).
- Swiss SMEs consciously using AI rose from **22% to 34%** in one year (AXA/Sotomo).

## Where AI hurts: what Swiss founding data shows

In 2023, the first full year after ChatGPT, new companies in industries highly exposed to AI **fell 1.3%**, while low-exposure industries **grew 7.1%**.

- **Doing businesses shrank:** office support (-16%), publishing (-15%), IT services (-7%), legal and accounting (-6%).
- **Advising and selling businesses grew:** advertising and market research (+16%), management consulting (+4%).

The catch: the growing sectors already had the **highest closure rates**, 9 to 10 per 100 companies a year, against 5.1 in health. [See it in the chart](/data/#ai).

## What gets dropped inside companies

- **95%** of organisations saw no measurable profit-and-loss return within six months (MIT NANDA, 2025).
- **42%** of companies dropped most of their AI initiatives in 2025, up from 17% a year earlier (S&P Global).
- About 60% of Swiss firms use AI, but only **2% of micro-firms** use it systematically (UBS).
- Only **34% of SMEs** have rules for what data staff may enter into AI tools.

## The lesson for small firms

AI is not yet the main reason companies fail. But for a small firm, **a badly chosen AI bet can itself be a survival risk**. Start with back-office tasks, put data rules in place, and keep the first experiments small and reversible.
