Every conversation about new companies now turns to AI. So I asked a narrower, testable question: **since ChatGPT, has AI changed which Swiss companies get founded, and does it affect how they survive?**

> AI's direct effect on five-year survival **cannot be proven yet**. Companies founded in 2023, the first full year after ChatGPT, reach year five around 2030. Everything below is strong circumstantial evidence, not proof.
{: .note}

## Where AI is boosting

Money and attention have moved fast. Adoption inside small companies has moved much more slowly.

## What the founding data shows

I compared new company foundings by industry in 2023 with 2022, and grouped industries by how exposed their work is to generative AI, based on the ILO's 2025 exposure index (the grouping is my judgement). Switch the chart to closure rates to see the second half of the story:

<div class="chart-embed" data-chart="ai" data-label="Change in new companies by industry, 2022 to 2023"></div>

- New companies in **highly exposed industries fell 1.3%**. In low-exposure industries they **rose 7.1%**. The national total was flat.
- **"Doing" businesses shrank:** office support (-16%), publishing (-15%), IT services (-7%), legal and accounting (-6%). This is work whose output AI can now produce.
- **"Advising and selling" businesses grew:** advertising and market research (+16%), management consulting (+4%). Cheap AI tools make it easy for one person to open such a business.

> The catch: the growing sectors already had the **highest closure rates**, 9 to 10 closures per 100 companies a year, against 5.1 in health. AI is pulling founders into the most fragile markets.
{: .key}

## The snowball effect

Looking across the evidence, the effects reinforce each other:

1. **Capital concentrates.** A few AI companies absorb a large share of funding, so non-AI startups get less money at lower valuations. Swiss fintech rounds fell from 41 in 2024 to 30 in 2025.
2. **Big models squeeze specialists.** Even DeepL, a Swiss-German AI translation champion, cut about 250 jobs. If the AI specialist is squeezed, traditional translation agencies are squeezed harder.
3. **Cheap tools crowd fragile markets.** More one-person consultancies and agencies, in sectors that already close most often.
4. **Badly chosen AI projects drain small firms**, which have no room for an expensive mistake.

## What gets dropped inside companies

- **95%** of organisations saw no measurable profit-and-loss return from generative AI pilots within six months (MIT NANDA, 2025).
- **42%** of companies abandoned most of their AI initiatives in 2025, up from 17% a year earlier (S&P Global).
- Only **34% of Swiss SMEs** have rules for what data staff may enter into AI tools, and only 23% of firms under ten employees (AXA/Sotomo).

The root cause is rarely the technology. It is where the money goes (often into sales and marketing pilots, while returns tend to show up in back-office automation), missing data foundations, and no clear owner.

## What I would tell a small firm

- **Start with the back office**: invoicing, scheduling, document handling. That is where returns show up.
- **Write data rules first**: what may and may not be pasted into AI tools.
- **Keep experiments small and reversible**, with a clear result that means stop. [Part 8](/blog/hidden-denominator-ai-success-rates/) explains why even a "70% success rate" is risky for a small company.
