**Part 3 of my series on why half of new Swiss companies close within five years.**

I expected regions with little venture capital to survive less. **Lucerne proved me wrong.**

## The numbers

- Lucerne had **one listed financing round** in 2025, of about CHF 125,000.
- Its five-year survival rose from **48.7% to 52.0%** between the 2013 and 2018 founding years, the biggest improvement of the five cantons.
- It had the **lowest closure rate** of the five cantons in 2021 (5.8 per 100 active companies).
- Zurich, with CHF 1.19 billion of venture capital, survives at 52.3%. Practically the same.

## Why money does not explain survival

- **Venture capital reaches very few companies.** About 350 financing rounds a year, against more than 46,000 new companies.
- **Most new companies never seek it.** Bakeries, consultancies, tradespeople and local services finance themselves differently.
- **Venture capital bets on fast growth, not survival.** Investors accept that most of their startups fail, as long as a few become very large.

## What probably keeps Lucerne's companies alive

Self-funding, bank loans and steady local customers. These are hypotheses: the official data shows the result, not the reason.

## The risk for Lucerne

Stability without growth. Few scale-ups means fewer high-value jobs. The opportunity is to add a growth layer, such as university spin-offs and regional investors, **without losing what already works**.
