I live in Lucerne, so this finding surprised me personally. In the Swiss Venture Capital Report 2026, Lucerne appears with **a single listed financing round** of about CHF 125,000. Zurich had 151 rounds worth CHF 1.19 billion.

If money drove survival, Lucerne's companies should struggle. Instead:

<div class="statrow">
  <div><b>52.0%</b><span>5-year survival in Lucerne (Zurich: 52.3%)</span></div>
  <div><b>+3.3 pts</b><span>improvement from the 2013 to the 2018 founding year, the largest of the five cantons</span></div>
  <div><b>5.8</b><span>closures per 100 active companies in 2021, the lowest of the five</span></div>
</div>

## The hypothesis I got wrong

Before looking at the data, I wrote down: *regions with little funding will have lower survival.* It seemed obvious. Lucerne is the clearest proof that it is wrong.

> A rejected hypothesis is a result, not a failure. Writing it down first is what stopped me from quietly forgetting it once the data disagreed.
{: .method}

## Why money and survival are not linked

**Venture capital reaches very few companies.** Swiss venture capital funds about 350 rounds a year. Switzerland founds more than 46,000 new companies a year. Most of them (bakeries, consultancies, tradespeople, clinics, shops) never look for venture capital at all.

**Venture capital bets on growth, not survival.** A venture investor expects most startups in a portfolio to fail, as long as a few become very large. Survival is not what the money is designed to buy.

**At sector level, the link even runs the other way.** Health-related startups received about 45% of Swiss venture capital in 2025, and health is the best-surviving sector. But that is because investors back a few science companies, not because funding keeps clinics and practices alive.

## So how do Lucerne's companies survive?

The official data shows the result, not the reason, so the following are hypotheses that would need interviews and financial data to confirm:

- **Self-funding and bank loans** instead of equity, which forces a business to be profitable early.
- **Steady local customers** in a stable regional economy.
- **Fewer cycle-driven sectors** such as crypto, which rise and fall together.

In consulting terms, Lucerne looks like a portfolio of low-risk, low-volatility businesses. That is exactly what you would expect to survive.

## The risk for Lucerne

Stability is not the same as growth. A canton with few venture-backed companies also has few scale-ups, and scale-ups create a large share of new high-value jobs. The opportunity is to add a growth layer, through university spin-offs, regional investors and founder support, **without losing the stability that already works**.

> Lucerne maximises durability, Zug maximises volume, and Zurich manages both. Each is a different strategy, not simply better or worse.
