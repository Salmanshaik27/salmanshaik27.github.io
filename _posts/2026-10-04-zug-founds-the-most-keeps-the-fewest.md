**Part 2 of my series on why half of new Swiss companies close within five years.**

Zug creates **13.7 new companies per 1,000 residents**, about 2.5 times Zurich's rate. Yet only **46.8%** of the companies founded there in 2018 still existed five years later, the lowest of the five cantons I compared.

## Not one bad year

Zug had the lowest five-year survival of the five cantons in **every founding year from 2013 to 2018**. This is a structural pattern, not bad luck. [Explore it year by year](/data/#cantons).

## Zug is a revolving door

In 2021, Zug had both the highest rate of new companies (10.3 per 100 active companies) and the highest closure rate (7.5 per 100). Companies arrive and leave faster than anywhere else.

## What does not explain it

- **Shell companies.** The BFS figures exclude holding companies and count only firms with real economic activity.
- **Size.** Zug has the *fewest* one-person starts (77% against 83% nationally), and small starts survive worst.
- **Money.** Zug receives more venture capital per resident than Zurich.
- **Crypto alone.** Zug has about 715 blockchain companies in total, against roughly 1,800 new companies every year.

## What probably does

- **Foreign-owned companies.** Nationally, foreign companies registered in Switzerland survive only 35% of the time, and Zug attracts many of them.
- **Mobile, tax-driven firms**, which can leave as easily as they arrive.
- **Cycle-driven sectors** such as crypto, which rise and fall together.

## The lesson

A canton that measures success by registrations celebrates the wrong number. **Survival and jobs** are the better scorecard.
