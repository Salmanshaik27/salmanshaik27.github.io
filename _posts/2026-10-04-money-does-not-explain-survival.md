**Part 6 of my series on why half of new Swiss companies close within five years.**

Before looking at the data, I wrote down my hypothesis: **regions with little funding survive less.** The data rejected it.

| Canton | VC per resident, 2025 | 5-year survival |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basel-Stadt | CHF 2,861 | 50.1% |
| Zug | CHF 2,026 | 46.8% |
| Zurich | CHF 745 | 52.3% |
| Bern | at least CHF 17 | 50.3% |
| Lucerne | CHF 0.3 | 52.0% |

## Where the money goes

In 2025, Swiss startups raised CHF 2,948 million. About **45% went to health-related startups** (biotech, medtech, healthcare IT) and about **34% to ICT and fintech**. AI startups took about a third of all venture capital, up from 5% two years earlier.

So at sector level, investors partly back the best survivors. At regional level, money and survival do not move together.

## Why a wrong hypothesis is a good result

Writing hypotheses down before looking at the data is what keeps an analysis honest. This one was wrong, and it turned into one of the most useful findings: **venture capital cannot explain the survival of companies it never touches.**
