In consulting, you are taught to state your hypothesis before you look at the data. Mine for this question was simple: **regions with little funding will have lower survival.** The data rejected it, and that turned into one of the most useful results of the study.

## The evidence

| Canton | VC per resident, 2025 | Rounds, 2025 | 5-year survival |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basel-Stadt | CHF 2,861 | 18 | 50.1% |
| Zug | CHF 2,026 | 30 | 46.8% |
| Zurich | CHF 745 | 151 | 52.3% |
| Bern | at least CHF 17 | 14 | 50.3% |
| Lucerne | CHF 0.3 | 1 | 52.0% |

The canton with the second-most venture capital per resident has the lowest survival. The canton with almost none ties with Zurich. There is no relationship.

> The venture capital report does not list Bern and Lucerne separately, so I rebuilt their figures from the report's list of individual financing rounds. They are minimums, which only strengthens the conclusion.
{: .method}

## Where Swiss venture capital goes

Swiss startups raised **CHF 2,948 million** in 2025 according to the Swiss Venture Capital Report. Switch the chart between sectors and cantons:

<div class="chart-embed" data-chart="vc" data-label="Venture capital by sector or canton"></div>

- About **45%** went to health-related startups (biotech CHF 946 million, medtech CHF 280 million, healthcare IT CHF 90 million).
- About **34%** went to ICT and fintech.
- AI startups took about **a third** of all Swiss venture capital, up from 5% two years earlier (EY Startup Barometer 2026).

So at sector level, investors partly back the best survivors. At regional level, money and survival simply do not move together.

## Why money cannot explain survival

**Scale.** About 350 financing rounds a year, against more than 46,000 new companies. Venture capital touches well under 1% of new Swiss companies.

**Purpose.** Venture capital is designed to fund a few companies that might grow very large, accepting that most will fail. It is a growth instrument, not a survival instrument.

**Concentration.** Money flows to a few hubs and a few sectors. The bakery, the IT consultancy and the plumbing company that make up most new companies are financed by savings, bank loans and customers.

> Venture capital cannot explain the survival of companies it never touches.
{: .key}

## What this means

For policymakers, attracting venture capital is a growth policy, not a survival policy. For founders, not having investors is not a disadvantage in itself; for most businesses, early customers and modest fixed costs matter far more. And for analysts, it is a reminder that the most visible part of the startup world is a small part of the economy.
