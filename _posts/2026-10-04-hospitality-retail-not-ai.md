Most of the public conversation about business failure is now about AI. But when I looked at which sectors actually lose the most new companies, the worst performers were restaurants, hotels and shops, **where AI barely plays a role**.

## Hospitality: a cost squeeze, not a lack of guests

Owners themselves name the problems: staff shortages, rising rents, changing habits (less alcohol, shorter lunches) and high staff costs. GastroSuisse describes margins that have been eroding for years. Chains cope better than independent restaurants because they can share staff across locations and buy in bulk.

<div class="rp-sec"><div class="cols">
<div class="card wrong"><h4>Where it goes wrong</h4><ol><li><b>The irreversible bet first:</b> a long lease and full fit-out before the concept is proven.</li><li><b>Stuck in the middle:</b> neither cheapest nor clearly special.</li><li><b>One person carries everything:</b> chef, manager and accountant.</li><li><b>No numbers:</b> nobody tracks cost per guest or staff cost share.</li></ol></div>
<div class="card move"><h4>How they could move</h4><ul><li><b>Test before signing:</b> pop-ups, shared kitchens, short leases.</li><li><b>Pick a clear position:</b> a focused niche instead of serving everyone.</li><li><b>Share fixed costs</b> with other owners: staff pools, joint purchasing.</li><li><b>Simple digital tools</b> for booking, scheduling and stock.</li></ul></div>
</div></div>

## Retail: squeezed from two sides

Swiss retail grew only **1.0%** in 2025, and non-food fell **2.7%** (NielsenIQ). Online sales reached **CHF 15.8 billion**, and 41% of retailers say they feel the pressure from Temu and Shein (Swiss Post). Temu alone handles around 50,000 Swiss orders a day. Meanwhile, shopping across the border remains a habit near the borders.

<div class="rp-sec"><div class="cols">
<div class="card wrong"><h4>Where it goes wrong</h4><ol><li><b>Fighting on price</b> against platforms with global scale.</li><li><b>Half-done digital:</b> a website without stock data, delivery or service behind it.</li><li><b>Generalist range:</b> nothing is a reason to visit.</li></ol></div>
<div class="card move"><h4>How they could move</h4><ul><li><b>Compete on what platforms cannot copy:</b> advice, fitting, repair, rental, local speed.</li><li><b>Use marketplaces as a channel</b>, not only as an enemy.</li><li><b>Narrow the range</b> to a niche and own it.</li></ul></div>
</div></div>

> The common mistake across both sectors is making the large, irreversible bet before the concept is proven. **Test small first.**
{: .remember}

## Why this is not an AI story

These businesses fail because of **costs, demand and competition**: the classic forces in [Porter's model](/blog/health-vs-hospitality-sectors/). That matters for policy. If the public debate focuses on AI, the support that would actually help restaurants and shops (lower fixed costs, shared services, cheaper testing of concepts) gets less attention.

## What to watch

- **Energy prices and the franc.** A stronger franc sends more Swiss shoppers and holidaymakers abroad.
- **Rules for foreign platforms.** The Council of States has called for stricter rules for Temu and Shein. If they come, non-food shops would get modest relief.
