**Part 5 of my series on why half of new Swiss companies close within five years.**

This is the clearest pattern in the whole dataset.

## The numbers

| Size at founding | Survival after 5 years |
| --- | --- |
| 1 employee | 49% |
| 2 to 4 employees | 59% |
| 5 to 9 employees | 59% |
| 10 or more employees | 68% |

About **83% of new Swiss companies start with one employee**. The weakest starting position is also the most common one.

## The big jump is from one to a small team

Going from one person to two to four employees adds **10 points** of survival. Going from two to four to five to nine adds nothing. The message is not "the bigger the better". It is **"do not start alone"**.

## Capital at the start helps too

- GmbH (needs CHF 20,000 of share capital): **56%** survive five years
- AG (needs CHF 100,000): **55%**
- Sole proprietorship (no capital needed): **48%**
- Foreign companies: **35%**

[See all legal forms](/data/#legal).

## Why teams survive

- **Capacity:** one person must sell, deliver, do the accounts and absorb every setback.
- **Complementary skills:** a product person plus a sales person.
- **Commitment:** hiring or putting in capital signals a better-prepared project.

A caution: this is correlation, not cause. Better-prepared founders may both hire more and survive more.
