If you want to predict whether a new Swiss company will still exist in five years, ask what it does before you ask where it is. **The gap between the best sector and the worst is 25 percentage points. The gap between the best canton and the worst is about 5.**

## The data

Change the founding year and the number of years in the chart below. The order of sectors barely moves, which tells me this is structural, not the effect of one bad year:

<div class="chart-embed" data-chart="sectors" data-label="Survival by sector"></div>

Two things stand out. First, health is far ahead of everything else. Second, the sectors that get the most attention, **ICT (46%) and finance (47%)**, survive below average. They are not the worst (trade, transport and hospitality do worse), but the hype clearly does not translate into durability.

## A consultant's lens: competitive pressure

To explain the gap, I used Michael Porter's Five Forces, a standard strategy tool that rates how much pressure a sector puts on the companies in it.

| Sector | 5-yr survival | Threat of new entrants | Rivalry | Substitutes | Overall pressure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hospitality | 38% | High | High | Medium | <span class="pill p-hi">Very high</span> |
| Trade and retail | 44% | High | High | High (online) | <span class="pill p-hi">Very high</span> |
| ICT | 46% | High | High | High (AI, offshore) | <span class="pill p-hi">High</span> |
| Construction | 51% | Medium | High | Low | <span class="pill p-md">Medium to high</span> |
| Health | 63% | Low (licences) | Medium | Low | <span class="pill p-lo">Low</span> |

*Force ratings are my own assessment, based on the facts in the study.*

> Health is the only sector where entry is restricted by qualifications and licences, and it has by far the highest survival. **Barriers to entry protect the companies that get in.**
{: .key}

## Why health survives

- **Few new entrants.** You cannot open a medical practice with a laptop and a website. Licences keep weak entrants out.
- **Stable demand.** People need healthcare in every economic cycle.
- **Low buyer power.** A large part of demand is paid through insurance, so patients do not haggle like retail customers.

## Why hospitality struggles

- **Anyone can enter.** Opening a café needs money, not a licence that filters for skill.
- **Costs rise faster than prices.** Restaurant revenue fell 4.8% in the first quarter of 2026 against a year earlier (GastroSuisse/KOF), while rents, wages and energy keep climbing.
- **The irreversible bet comes first.** A long lease and a full fit-out are signed before the concept is proven. [Part 10](/blog/hospitality-retail-not-ai/) goes deeper.

## Why the hype sectors disappoint

ICT and finance have low entry barriers too: anyone can open an IT or financial consulting company. They also depend on investor mood. Swiss fintech financing rounds fell from 41 in 2024 to 30 in 2025, and when funding dries up, young companies in these sectors run out of runway.

> Hype does not bring durability. The honest message is not "tech is the worst place to start", but "the sectors that get the most attention do not produce the most durable companies".
{: .remember}

## What this means for a founder

Before choosing a market, ask the questions an investor or a consultant would ask: **What stops the next ten competitors from copying me? Will demand be there in a recession? Who has the bargaining power, me or my customers?** A market with a real barrier, whether a licence, a hard-to-earn skill, a trusted relationship or a regulation, is worth more than a fashionable one.
